A battery that no longer holds a charge, an SSD that has started to fail or a hinge that has cracked can each be replaced, and the laptop goes back to how it was. Below is every laptop problem we see in the workshop, grouped by what is going wrong, with what causes it, how urgent it is and the fixed price. Not sure which one yours is? Laptop batteries wear out with every charge cycle. After a few years the charge that used to last a day can be gone in an hour, the percentage jumps around, or the laptop only runs while it is plugged in.

A swollen battery is the one to treat as urgent. It pushes the trackpad up, bends the case or lifts the keyboard, and it should not be charged or left plugged in. Bring it in and we replace it straight away.

We fit a battery made for your model and test it before you collect the laptop. Most major brands are supported. We do not replace batteries on MacBook or Surface devices.

A charger that only works when the cable is held a certain way usually comes down to the port. Plugging in and out every day wears it, and a tug on the cable can crack it or break it off the board.

We repair or replace the port on laptops and 2-in-1 devices, including USB-C charging ports. If it turns out to be the charger rather than the laptop, we tell you, so you only buy what you need.

A drop, a knock or something left on the keyboard when the lid was closed can crack the panel. A crack with a clear image behind it can wait a little. Black patches, colour bleeding across the image or a screen that has gone dark mean the panel itself is damaged, and that tends to spread.

Laptop panels are not one universal part. Size, resolution, refresh rate, touch and the connector all vary, sometimes between two versions of the same model. We confirm the exact panel your laptop takes and give you the full price, panel and labour included, before we start. Then we fit it and test it before you collect.

When the problem changes as you move the lid, the panel is often fine. The display cable runs through the hinge and wears where it flexes, or its connector works loose. Repairing that is a smaller job than a new screen, and usually cheaper.

We test which it is before quoting, so you are not sold a new panel when the fault was the cable.

Worth booking in while it is only loose. We repair or replace the hinge and check the mounts and cable while the laptop is open.

# ---- Blue screens, crashes and Windows --------------------------------- - id: blue-screen group: "Blue screens, crashes and Windows" title: "Blue screens, crashing and random restarts" price_key: diagnostic-repair-all-in signs: - "Blue screen with a stop code" - "Restarts on its own, often under load" - "Freezes and needs a hard power off" - "Crashes started after an update or a new program" text: |

A blue screen, the "blue screen of death", is Windows stopping itself to prevent damage. It happens once in a while on most computers. When it keeps happening, something is wrong, and the stop code on its own rarely tells you what.

The common causes are a failing SSD, faulty memory, a driver that clashes with an update, overheating, or corrupted Windows files. Some are software fixes and some need a part, which is why we test the hardware before touching Windows. Guessing wrong costs you twice.

The all-in diagnostic finds the cause and covers the repair labour. If the fault is a part, such as memory or the drive, we quote it before fitting it. If it turns out to be Windows itself, the Windows repair and reinstall page covers that.

A laptop that powers on but never reaches the desktop has usually lost part of Windows, often after an update that did not finish, a power cut mid-update or a drive that is starting to fail.

# ---- Heat, noise and spills -------------------------------------------- - id: overheating group: "Heat, noise and spills" title: "Overheating, fan noise and laptop cleaning" price_key: overheating-fix-deep-clean also_keys: - gaming-laptop-repaste-clean signs: - "Fan loud even on light work" - "Too hot to rest on your lap" - "Slows down or shuts off under load" - "Rattling or grinding fan" text: |

Dust packs into the fan and the cooling fins, and the thermal paste between the processor and the heatsink dries out. The laptop runs hot, the fan runs flat out, and it slows itself down to cope or shuts off to protect itself.

A proper laptop clean means taking it apart, not blowing air through the vents. We strip it down, clean the fan and cooling fins, replace the thermal paste and check the fan itself. Temperatures come back down and the noise with them. Gaming laptops run hotter and take longer to strip down, so they have their own price.

- id: liquid-spill group: "Heat, noise and spills" title: "Liquid spill clean and repair" price_key: liquid-spill-clean-repair text: |

Turn it off, unplug it and bring it in as soon as you can. Do not charge it to see if it works. Liquid keeps working its way in and causes corrosion over the following days, even when the laptop still seems fine.

We strip the laptop down, clean the inside and treat any corrosion before it spreads. If a part has already been damaged, such as the keyboard or the board, we tell you what it is and what it costs before we go further.

sections: - id: repair-or-replace title: "Repair or replace?" html: |

Most of the time, repair. A new battery, port, keyboard or SSD costs a fraction of a new laptop, and a laptop that was working well before it broke will usually keep working well for years after.

Replacing makes more sense when the repair would cost close to what the laptop is worth, when it was already too slow for how you use it, or when several things have gone at once. If that is the case, we say so honestly before any repair work starts.
