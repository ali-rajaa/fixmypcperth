Microsoft, Apple, your bank and your internet provider will never show a pop-up asking you to call them, and will never ring you out of the blue to fix your computer. Those warnings and calls are scams. The pop-up itself is usually just a web page and does no damage, as long as you do not call the number.

If you did call, and let someone connect to your computer, treat it as urgent. Do not use that computer for banking. Contact your bank from your phone, and change your email and banking passwords from a different device. Then bring the computer in.

We remove the remote access programs the scammer installed, along with anything else they left behind, and check for changes they made to your accounts and settings.

We get you back in to your Microsoft or Windows account, change the password, and help you turn on two-step sign-in so a stolen password on its own is not enough. If the computer itself is infected, the clean-up is priced separately.

- id: real-or-fake title: "Is that virus warning real?" html: |

Real protection, such as Windows Security, shows its alerts in the corner of the screen, names the file it found, and deals with it itself. It never asks you to phone anyone, pay by gift card or download a separate program to fix it.

A warning that fills the whole browser, plays a sound, shows a phone number or says your computer will be locked is a scam page. Close the browser. If it will not close, turn the computer off. If you already called the number or paid, see the section on tech support scams above.
