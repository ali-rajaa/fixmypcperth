A slow computer is the most common thing people bring in, and the least specific. The same slowness can come from an old hard drive, too little memory, dust and dried thermal paste, malware running in the background, or years of programs that all start with Windows. Each has a different fix at a different price.

That is why we find the cause before selling you anything. A tune up will not help a failing drive, and a new SSD will not help a computer that is overheating. You get told what is actually slowing it down, what fixes it, and what it costs, then you decide.

Not sure what is wrong? The all-in diagnostic is a flat [[diagnostic-repair-all-in]] that finds the cause and covers the repair labour. If you would rather start with a clean-up, the tune ups below are fixed prices too.

# ---- Hardware that slows it down --------------------------------------- - id: ssd group: "Hardware that slows it down" title: "Old hard drive: SSD upgrade" price_key: hdd-to-ssd-full-migration also_keys: - ssd-upgrade-labour-only signs: - "Several minutes to start up" - "Drive light on constantly" - "Everything waits a few seconds before it responds" - "Clicking or whirring from inside" text: |

Plenty of computers sold only a few years ago still came with a mechanical hard drive. It is the single most common reason a computer feels slow at everything, because every task waits on the drive first. Swapping it for an SSD is the biggest speed difference you can make to an older computer.

We supply the SSD and clone your whole system across, so it starts up just as before with all your files and programs, only much faster. If you already have the drive, the labour-only price is in the table below. Every upgrade we fit, from SSDs to graphics cards, is on the computer upgrades page. - id: ram group: "Hardware that slows it down" title: "Not enough memory: RAM upgrade" price_key: ram-upgrade-supplied also_keys: - ram-upgrade-labour-only signs: - "Slows right down with a few tabs open" - "Freezes when switching between programs" - "Fine after a restart, slow again an hour later" - "Struggles with video calls" text: |

Browsers, video calls and modern programs use far more memory than they did a few years ago. When there is not enough, the computer falls back on the drive, and everything slows down.

We check how much memory the computer has, how much it is using, and what it can take, then source and fit the right RAM. Some thin laptops have memory soldered to the board, and we say so rather than sell an upgrade that cannot be fitted.

- id: overheating group: "Hardware that slows it down" title: "Dust and overheating: deep clean" price_key: overheating-fix-deep-clean also_keys: - cpu-thermal-paste-replacement signs: - "Fans loud even on light work" - "Fast at first, then slows down after a while" - "Hot to touch around the vents" - "Shuts off under load" text: |

When a processor gets too hot, it slows itself down to protect itself. Dust packed into the fans and dried-out thermal paste are the usual causes, and Perth summers make it worse. The computer is fast for the first few minutes, then drops off.

A proper computer clean means taking it apart, not blowing air through the vents. We strip it down, clean the fans and heatsinks, replace the thermal paste and check the fans, so it can run at full speed again.

- id: failing-drive group: "Hardware that slows it down" title: "A failing drive" price_key: diagnostic-repair-all-in signs: - "Freezes for seconds at a time" - "Files that take forever to open, or will not" - "Slowness getting worse week by week" - "Error messages about the disk" text: |

A drive that is starting to fail often shows up first as slowness, long before it stops working. It keeps retrying the parts it cannot read, and everything waits. This one is worth dealing with quickly, because it is the part that holds your files.

The diagnostic confirms whether the drive is failing. If it is, we replace it and move your data across while it can still be read. Our guide to the signs a hard drive is failing explains how urgent each one is, and the data recovery page covers getting files off a drive that has already gone.

Most slow computers are worth fixing. An SSD, more memory or a proper clean costs a fraction of a new computer, and a machine that was fine a few years ago can usually be made fast again.

A new computer makes more sense when it is very old, when it cannot take an SSD or more memory, or when it is also failing in other ways.
