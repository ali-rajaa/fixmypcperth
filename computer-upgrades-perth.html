Most computers that feel too slow are not worn out. They are held back by one or two parts: an old mechanical hard drive, not enough memory for how it is used now, or a graphics card that can no longer keep up with the games. Upgrading the right part costs a fraction of a new computer, and everything you have on it stays where it is.

The trick is knowing which part. An upgrade in the wrong place does nothing, and a computer that is slow because it is overheating or infected will not be fixed by more memory. So we start with what you use it for, what it has now and what it can take, and recommend the upgrade that makes the biggest difference for your money.

Upgrade advice is free: message us your specs and budget and we tell you what is worth doing before you spend anything. Fitting is a fixed price, whether you buy the part yourself or we supply it.

# ---- Memory upgrades --------------------------------------------------- - id: ram group: "Memory upgrades" title: "RAM upgrade" price_key: ram-upgrade-supplied also_keys: - ram-upgrade-labour-only signs: - "Slows right down with several tabs open" - "Freezes when switching between programs" - "Video calls stutter" - "Fine after a restart, slow again later" text: |

Browsers, video calls and modern programs use far more memory than they did a few years ago. With too little, the computer falls back on the drive and everything slows down. More RAM lets it keep more open at once without struggling.

We check how much memory your computer has, how much it actually uses, and the maximum and type it takes, then supply and fit the right RAM, DDR4 or DDR5, with XMP or EXPO switched on in the BIOS so it runs at its full rated speed. If you have already bought the RAM, the labour-only price covers fitting and configuring it.

Some thin laptops have memory soldered to the board. We check yours first and tell you if that is the case, rather than sell an upgrade that cannot be fitted.

A processor upgrade is possible when the motherboard supports a faster chip, and on some desktops it is a good-value way to add years of life. On others the board, the memory and the processor would all need to change together, which is closer to a new build.

Tell us the motherboard or the computer's model and we tell you what it can take and whether it is worth doing, before you spend anything. Laptop processors are soldered to the board and cannot be upgraded.

sections: - id: what-to-upgrade title: "Which upgrade will help most?" html: |
  • Slow at everything, especially starting up: an SSD, if it still has a mechanical hard drive.
  • Slows down with lots open, or on video calls: more RAM.
  • Games run at low frame rates: the graphics card, usually, then the processor.
  • Fast at first, then slows down, or very loud: better cooling, or a clean and repaste.
  • Out of space: a larger SSD or a second drive.

If it has become slow suddenly rather than gradually, an upgrade is probably not the answer. That is more often malware, a failing drive or a Windows problem, and we would check for those first.

- id: upgrade-or-replace title: "Upgrade or buy a new computer?" html: |

Upgrade when the computer is basically sound and one or two parts are holding it back. An SSD and more memory can make a computer from several years ago feel new again for a fraction of the price of a replacement.

Replace when it is old enough that it cannot take an SSD or more memory, when the processor is the bottleneck on a laptop, or when it is also failing in other ways. If yours is at that point, we tell you, and can move your files across to the new one for [[pc-to-pc-data-transfer]].

Laptops can be upgraded too, but what is possible depends on the model. Most can take an SSD. Many can take more memory, though some thin laptops have it soldered in place. The processor and graphics are almost always fixed. We check your exact model before recommending anything, and for laptop repairs as well as upgrades, see the laptop repair page.
