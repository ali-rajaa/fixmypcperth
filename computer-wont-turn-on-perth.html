A computer that will not turn on feels like the worst outcome, but it is often less serious than it looks. The fault is usually in how the computer gets power or how it starts up, not in the drive that holds your files. A failed power supply, a dead charger or battery, a loose connection, a memory stick that has worked loose or a Windows start-up that has broken can all look like a dead computer.

What it is doing tells us a lot. Nothing at all points one way, lights and fans with no picture another, and a logo that never gets further another again. Below is each of those, what it usually means and what it costs to fix.

Finding the cause is the all-in diagnostic, a flat [[diagnostic-repair-all-in]] that covers the diagnosis and all the repair labour, never an hourly rate. If a part is needed, such as a power supply, charger or battery, we tell you what it is and what it costs before we fit it.

" repairs: # ---- No power at all --------------------------------------------------- - id: desktop-no-power group: "No power at all" title: "Desktop PC won't turn on" price_key: diagnostic-repair-all-in signs: - "No lights or fans when you press power" - "Clicks once, then nothing" - "Died during a storm or power cut" - "Smell of burning from the back" text: |

On a desktop, no power at all is most often the power supply, the box at the back that the power cable plugs into. They wear out, and storms, surges and heat speed that up. It can also be the power button, a loose internal cable, or less often the motherboard.

We test the power supply and each connection, find what has failed, and replace it. If there was a burning smell, unplug it and do not try it again before bringing it in.

- id: laptop-no-power group: "No power at all" title: "Laptop won't turn on or charge" price_key: diagnostic-repair-all-in also_keys: - charging-port-repair - laptop-battery-replacement signs: - "Nothing happens, plugged in or not" - "Charging light off, or flashing" - "Only turns on while plugged in" - "Charger gets hot or the plug is loose" text: |

A laptop that shows no sign of life is usually one of three things: the charger, the charging port, or the battery. A charger can fail without looking any different, a port wears loose, and a completely flat or failed battery can stop the laptop starting even when it is plugged in.

We test each one and fix the one that has failed. A charging port repair and a battery replacement are both fixed prices, shown below. If it is none of those, the diagnostic finds what is. For every other laptop fault, see laptop repair.

# ---- Powers on, but no picture or no start ----------------------------- - id: no-display group: "Powers on, but no picture or no start" title: "Turns on but no display" price_key: diagnostic-repair-all-in signs: - "Fans spin and lights come on, screen stays black" - "Monitor says \"no signal\"" - "Beeps when it starts" - "Keyboard lights come on, then nothing" text: |

When a computer powers on but never shows a picture, it is failing its own start-up checks before Windows is even involved. The usual causes are memory that has worked loose or failed, the graphics card, a display cable, or on a laptop the screen itself. Beeps or flashing lights at start-up are the computer's way of saying which part it is unhappy with.

We work through them in order, reseating and testing each part, and fix or replace the one that has failed. On a laptop, if the picture works on an external monitor, the laptop screen repair page covers it.

- id: turns-off group: "Powers on, but no picture or no start" title: "Turns on, then turns straight off" price_key: diagnostic-repair-all-in signs: - "Starts for a second or two, then dies" - "Keeps restarting in a loop" - "Fans spin up, then stop" - "Shuts down after a few minutes" text: |

A computer that starts and then cuts out is usually protecting itself. It might be overheating, often from a cooler that has come loose or dried-out paste, a power supply that cannot hold up once the computer starts drawing power, or a short somewhere inside.

This one is worth bringing in rather than trying again and again, since repeated attempts can make a power or heat fault worse. We find the cause and fix it.

- id: stuck-logo group: "Powers on, but no picture or no start" title: "Stuck on the logo or won't boot into Windows" price_key: diagnostic-repair-all-in also_keys: - windows-reinstall-data-backup signs: - "Stuck on the manufacturer's logo" - "Spinning circle that never finishes" - "\"No bootable device\" message" - "Automatic Repair loop" text: |

If you see the manufacturer's logo, the hardware is mostly working, and the problem is getting Windows started. The causes are a damaged Windows start-up, often after an interrupted update, or a drive that is failing.

We check the drive first. If it is failing, we replace it and move your files across. If it is healthy, we repair Windows or reinstall it with your files backed up first. The Windows reinstall page has the detail.

# ---- After an accident ------------------------------------------------- - id: spill group: "After an accident" title: "Won't turn on after a spill" price_key: liquid-spill-clean-repair text: |

Turn it off, unplug it, and do not try to turn it on or charge it to see if it works. Power running through a wet board is what turns a spill into real damage. Bring it in as soon as you can. We strip it down, clean it and treat any corrosion, then tell you what, if anything, has been damaged and what it costs.

- id: storm group: "After an accident" title: "Won't turn on after a storm or power surge" price_key: diagnostic-repair-all-in text: |

A lightning strike nearby or a surge when the power comes back can take out a power supply, a charger or a network port, and occasionally more. Often it is only the power supply or charger that has failed, and replacing it brings the computer back. We test each part and tell you what the surge reached.

- id: drop group: "After an accident" title: "Won't turn on after a drop" price_key: diagnostic-repair-all-in text: |

A drop can loosen or break an internal connection, crack a screen so it looks dead, damage the charging port or, on older laptops, damage a mechanical hard drive. We open it up, check what the impact reached and fix it. If the drive was damaged, your files come first.

# ---- Your files -------------------------------------------------------- - id: files group: "Your files" title: "Getting your files off a computer that won't start" price_key: data-recovery-failing-drive text: |

A computer that will not turn on almost never means your files are gone. The drive is usually fine even when the power supply, the motherboard or the screen has failed. If the computer is worth repairing, the files stay on it. If it is not, we take the drive out and recover your files, whether the rest of the computer is fixable or not.

If the drive itself has failed, that is a data recovery job, and on hard drive recovery you pay nothing if we cannot get your files back. The data recovery page has the detail.

sections: - id: right-now title: "Before you bring it in" html: |
  • Check it is not something simple: the wall switch is on, the power cable is pushed fully into the computer, and a laptop charger's light comes on.
  • If there was a spill, a burning smell or a storm, unplug it and do not keep trying to turn it on.
  • Do not open it up to look inside. It rarely helps and it can make a power fault worse.
  • Note what it does when you press power: nothing, lights only, fans and no picture, beeps, or a logo that goes no further. It speeds up the diagnosis.
  • Bring the charger if it is a laptop, and the power cable if it is a desktop.

No appointment is needed. The workshop takes drop-offs around the clock, so there is no waiting until morning.

- id: repair-or-replace title: "Is it worth fixing?" html: |

Most of the time, yes. A power supply, charger, battery, memory or charging port is a small part of what the computer is worth, and once it is replaced the computer works as before.

It is worth thinking twice when the motherboard has failed on an older computer, since that can cost close to what the computer is worth. If that is where yours is, we tell you before any repair work starts, and your files can still be recovered and moved to a new computer for [[pc-to-pc-data-transfer]].
