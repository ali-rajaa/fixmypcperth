Why is my computer so slow? The warning signs

A slow computer is often more than an annoyance. It can be the first sign of a part starting to fail. These are the signs we see most on the bench, and what each one usually means:

  • Start-up taking a minute or more. Often an old mechanical hard drive near the end of its life. Drives usually slow down before they fail.
  • Freezing, stuttering or "Not Responding" in normal use. Often memory or storage, and it tends to get worse once it starts.
  • Fans loud all the time, or the case hot to touch. Dust stops the cooling working, so the processor slows itself down to stay safe. Running hot for long periods also shortens the life of the parts, and Perth summers make it worse.
  • Browsing slow even with nothing else open. Can be malware or adware running in the background, which may also be collecting passwords and card details.
  • Programs crashing or files that will not open. A later sign. Losing files is now a real risk, and recovery gets harder the longer the computer keeps running.
  • Blue screens, random restarts or sudden shutdowns. Usually unstable hardware. Carrying on using it can turn a simple repair into a data recovery job.
  • Windows updates taking longer, or failing. Usually corrupted system files. A computer that cannot update also misses its security fixes.

Many people live with a slow computer for months and bring it in the week it stops, by which point a same-day repair has become a data recovery job.

If your computer shows any of these signs, the cheapest time to have it looked at is while it still turns on. The diagnostic tells you what is happening before you commit to anything, and most jobs booked before 2pm are ready the same day.

Signs your computer has a virus

Modern malware is built to stay hidden, so the signs are often small: a browser homepage or search engine that changed on its own, toolbars or extensions you did not install, pop-ups when you are not browsing, programs opening or closing by themselves, antivirus switched off, or friends getting messages "from you" that you never sent.

An infection keeps working for as long as it stays on the computer. It can record what you type, collect saved passwords and card details, and spread to other devices on the same network, and some ransomware waits weeks before it locks your files. If you see any of these signs, have it looked at soon rather than later.

Data recovery: the warning signs

If you think a drive is failing, treat it as urgent. A failing drive gets worse each time it starts, and the chance of getting files back drops with it. Signs to watch for: clicking, grinding or beeping, files that will not open or look corrupted, freezing while copying files, folders that look empty when they should not, and more frequent blue screens.

If the drive is making a noise, turn the computer off and bring it in. A drive that is restarted again and again while it clicks can reach the point where nothing can be recovered. Caught early, recovery is usually straightforward.
