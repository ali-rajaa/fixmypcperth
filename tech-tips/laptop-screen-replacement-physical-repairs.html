This is the most common kind of screen damage: a drop or knock has cracked the outer glass or the panel itself, but the image underneath is still complete and readable through the damage. Structurally, the screen is still doing its job, just with a visible crack across it.

It is the one situation here that genuinely does not need to be rushed. A cracked but functioning screen can be used carefully for a while, though a spreading crack or loose glass is worth keeping an eye on, since either can turn a cosmetic issue into the screen no longer working at all.

- id: lines-or-bleeding title: "Coloured lines, patches of black, or the image bleeding across the screen" urgency: book-this-week urgency_label: "Book this week" price_key: laptop-screen-replacement text: |

Lines running across the display, blotches of colour that should not be there, or the image smearing into areas that should be a different colour usually mean the impact reached the display panel itself, not just the glass in front of it. This is a step beyond a cosmetic crack.

The damage in a panel like this does not repair itself and tends to spread rather than stay the same, with more of the display affected as time goes on. It is not the most urgent sign on this page, but it is worth booking in before more of the screen is affected.

- id: dark-screen title: "The screen has gone completely dark, but the laptop is still running" urgency: book-this-week urgency_label: "Book this week" price_key: laptop-screen-replacement text: |

A laptop that is clearly powered on, with the keyboard lights active and fans running, but shows nothing at all on the screen, points to the display or its backlight rather than the rest of the machine. This is common after an impact even without a visible crack, since the connection between the screen and the rest of the laptop can be knocked loose or damaged without the glass itself breaking.

Because the rest of the computer is working normally, this is very often a screen-only repair rather than anything more serious. The main risk is continuing to use the laptop blind to a fault that might be getting worse, so it is worth having looked at sooner rather than assuming the whole machine has failed.

- id: hinge-damage title: "The screen is loose, wobbling, or coming away from the hinge" urgency: stop-now urgency_label: "Stop using it now" price_key: laptop-screen-replacement text: |

The cable connecting the screen to the rest of the laptop runs through the hinge, and a hinge that is loose, cracked, or letting the screen wobble puts that cable under repeated stress every time the lid opens or closes. What starts as a mechanical fault in the hinge can end with the display cable itself damaged.

This is the one kind of screen damage that reliably gets worse with ordinary use, because every open and close cycle is another chance for the cable to be pinched or pulled. A laptop in this state is worth taking out of daily use until it is looked at, rather than opened and closed as normal while waiting for a convenient time.

- id: liquid-damage title: "Liquid got into the screen and left marks, discolouration or dead patches" urgency: stop-now urgency_label: "Stop using it now" price_key: laptop-screen-replacement text: |

A splash that reaches the display can leave visible marks straight away, or the damage can spread gradually over the following days as moisture works its way further into the panel. Patches that were not there when it happened, or ones that grow larger over time, are both signs the panel is still being affected.

Liquid damage does not stop once the laptop has dried out. Corrosion inside a display can continue for some time afterward, so a screen affected this way is worth having looked at promptly rather than waiting to see whether it settles down on its own.
