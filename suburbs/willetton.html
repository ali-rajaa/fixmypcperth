Willetton is a family suburb in the fullest sense. Around 84 per cent of its households are families, the median age is about 40, and within its 8.6 square kilometres sit Willetton Primary, Rostrata Primary, Burrendah Primary and Willetton Senior High School. A lot of what happens on a Willetton computer is homework, assignments and study.

What that means for repairs is timing. A laptop failing on a quiet Saturday is an inconvenience. So the faults that matter most in Willetton are the ones that take a student's work with them: a drink across the keyboard, a battery that dies mid-lesson, and files that only ever existed on one USB stick.

In Willetton the laptop is often a student's most important possession, and it lives a hard life. It goes in a school bag every morning, gets opened on bus seats and desks, and runs for hours on battery. Faults tend to arrive in a predictable order: first the battery stops lasting the day, then a key or two gives up, then something gets spilled on it.

Most of those are quick to fix and none of them mean buying a new laptop. The one that needs speed is a spill. Liquid does more damage the longer it sits, so a laptop that has had a drink across it should be switched off and brought in as soon as possible rather than left to dry on the bench.

If the laptop is needed for school the next day, say so when you drop it off. We will tell you up front whether the fix can be done in time.

desktops_intro: |

The family desktop in a Willetton home is usually the shared machine: one person's study, another's work from home, someone else's games in the evening. That makes it the computer that fills up fastest, with three people's programs, downloads and files all competing for the same space.

When a shared machine slows down, the fix is often a clean-up and an upgrade rather than a replacement: clearing out what nobody uses, adding memory, and moving to faster storage. It is also worth giving each person their own account so one user's clutter stops slowing everyone else down.

data_intro: |

For students, lost data has a deadline attached. An assignment that disappears a week before it is due is a very different problem from one that disappears the night before. The most common way it happens is not a failed hard drive but a single copy on a USB stick, which gets lost, stood on, or simply stops being recognised one day.

The fix is dull and completely effective: school work saved somewhere that copies itself automatically, so there is always a second copy nobody had to remember to make. Set it up once at the start of the school year and it looks after itself.
