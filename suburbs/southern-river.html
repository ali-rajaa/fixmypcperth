why_local: |

Southern River was farmland until well into the 1990s, home to egg producers, horse studs and boarding kennels rather than housing. What is there now is almost entirely newer: new estates are still being released today, and the suburb has been named among the fastest-growing in Perth for several years running, with around 12,800 residents as of the last census and more arriving with every new release.

That newness changes what we are asked to look at compared with an older suburb. Very few Southern River machines are failing simply because they are old. What we see far more of is Wi-Fi that will not reach the back of a modern double-storey home, brand new houses that need their network set up properly from the first day, and laptops belonging to people working or studying from home that have quietly slowed down under the weight of everything installed on them over a few years, not from age but from clutter.

The first wave of Southern River's boom, homes bought in the early 2000s, is now old enough that its computers are starting to show the same ageing-drive symptoms as an established suburb. That group is growing, but it still sits alongside a much larger share of newer households whose problems are about setup and software rather than hardware wearing out.

Laptops make up the majority of what comes to us from Southern River, which fits a suburb built mostly in the last twenty years and full of people working or studying from home. The dominant complaint is not damage or age, it is a machine that has slowly become sluggish under years of browser extensions, startup programs and cloud sync tools that were each installed for a good reason at the time.

A tune-up clears that clutter out without touching anything physical, and it is usually enough on its own. Where it is not, the next most common laptop job is a battery that no longer holds charge through a working day, which shows up earlier on machines used constantly for video calls and remote desktop sessions than it would on a laptop used occasionally.

If the laptop looks dead, bring the charger along with it. It happens often enough that the charger, not the laptop, turns out to be the actual fault, and having it with us rules that out in minutes rather than after we have opened the machine up.

desktops_intro: |

Desktops from Southern River tend to fall into two groups: gaming machines built or bought for a family member, and the smaller but growing number of older towers from the suburb's first housing wave in the early 2000s. The gaming machines mostly come in for heat, a case that has never been cleaned out running hot enough under load to shut itself down or crash mid-session, which a clean and fresh thermal paste generally resolves in an afternoon.

The older towers are a more familiar story: a mechanical drive that has slowed the whole machine down over the years. Migrating it to an SSD makes more difference to how it feels day to day than any other single change, and it costs a fraction of replacing the computer.

Cannot get the tower into the car? We visit on-site across Southern River, and anything that does not need hands physically on the machine can usually be sorted remotely instead.

data_intro: |

Losing the only copy of something is the conversation nobody wants to have, and it comes up more than you would think: a semester of study notes, years of tax records, photos nobody thought to back up anywhere else. What comes back depends on the drive itself. Still spinning but struggling gives genuinely good odds, particularly if it is imaged before it gets worse. Stopped entirely is a harder case, and the success rate drops accordingly.

Either way, switching the machine off the moment files start disappearing matters more than anything else you can do, since every extra minute it runs is a chance to overwrite what is still there. For a household still setting itself up, it is also worth having a proper backup running before this ever becomes a problem.

local_area: |

Southern River borders Canning Vale directly to the north and west, with Huntingdale and Gosnells to the north east, Harrisdale and Piara Waters to the south west and Forrestdale and Armadale to the south. The CBD is about 22 kilometres north-west.

The suburb has its own shopping centre on Ranford Road at Bristile Avenue, anchored by a Coles, with Bunnings Harrisdale directly opposite and Livingston Shopping Centre in Canning Vale a short drive further. Southern Grove Primary School, Ashburton Drive Primary School and Southern River College are all close to Ranford Road, and Sutherlands Park and the reserves around it give the suburb much of its open space.
