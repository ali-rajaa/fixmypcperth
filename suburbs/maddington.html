why_local: |

Maddington is unusual among the suburbs we cover in that it never fully stopped being what it was before Perth's south-east grew around it. Alongside the roughly 12,400 residents, the suburb still holds working orchards and vineyards left from its agricultural past, a stretch of light industrial land, and its own train station on the Armadale line. It is genuinely mixed-use in a way most purely residential suburbs are not.

That mix shows up directly in what we are asked to fix. A good share of Maddington machines belong to tradies and small operators, an invoicing laptop that lives in the ute, a desktop running the books for a business on the light industrial side of the suburb, and they cannot sit in a queue the way a family desktop can. Alongside that is the more familiar pattern of an established residential suburb: family homes that have owned the same computer for years, now running noticeably slower than it used to.

Both groups tend to assume the whole machine is the problem when it is usually one part of it. A tired mechanical drive dragging down an otherwise fine computer is common, and replacing just the drive is nearly always cheaper than replacing the machine.

A larger share of Maddington's laptops are work tools than we see in a purely residential suburb, which changes what actually matters when one comes in. A tradie's laptop going down mid-week is not just an inconvenience, it stops jobs being quoted and invoices going out, so these get treated as priority jobs rather than queued behind whatever else is on the bench.

Outside of work machines, the usual wear-and-tear faults apply: batteries that no longer hold a charge, fans that have become loud enough to notice, and the occasional cracked screen from a laptop that travels between the house and a job site. Physical damage is priced on the part, so we confirm the exact component before ordering it.

If a laptop looks dead, bring the charger with it. A failed charger gets mistaken for a dead laptop often enough that having yours on the bench rules it out in minutes.

desktops_intro: |

Maddington's older housing means a genuine share of its desktops are ten years old or more, carrying a mechanical drive that has slowed the whole machine down without anyone quite noticing how much until it is replaced. Migrating to an SSD is usually the single biggest improvement available, and it costs a fraction of a new computer.

Heat is the other recurring fault, especially on towers that have sat under a desk collecting dust for years. Once the thermal paste dries out and the heatsink clogs, the machine starts shutting itself down under any real load. A clean and fresh paste generally sorts it out in an afternoon.

For a small business machine that genuinely cannot leave the premises, on-site visits are available across Maddington, and remote support covers anything that does not need hands physically on the machine.

data_intro: |

Some of the hardest calls we take are from people who assumed their files were safe until a drive proved otherwise: family photos with no second copy, or the invoicing history for a small business that has been trading for years. What can be pulled back depends on the state the drive is in. One that still spins but is struggling gives good odds, particularly if it is imaged early. One that has stopped spinning entirely is a harder job, and the odds drop from there.

Whichever it turns out to be, switching the machine off the moment files start disappearing matters more than anything else you can do, since every extra minute it runs is a chance to overwrite what is still there.
