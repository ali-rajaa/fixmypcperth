why_local: |

Maddington is unusual among the suburbs we cover in that it never fully stopped being what it was before Perth's south-east grew around it. Alongside the roughly 12,400 residents, the suburb still holds working orchards and vineyards left from its agricultural past, a stretch of light industrial land, and its own train station on the Armadale line. It is genuinely mixed-use in a way most purely residential suburbs are not.

That mix shows up directly in what we are asked to fix. A good share of Maddington machines belong to tradies and small operators, an invoicing laptop that lives in the ute, a desktop running the books for a business on the light industrial side of the suburb, and they cannot sit in a queue the way a family desktop can. Alongside that is the more familiar pattern of an established residential suburb: family homes that have owned the same computer for years, now running noticeably slower than it used to.

Both groups tend to assume the whole machine is the problem when it is usually one part of it. A tired mechanical drive dragging down an otherwise fine computer is common, and replacing just the drive is nearly always cheaper than replacing the machine.

In Maddington the laptop is as likely to ride in a ute as sit on a kitchen bench. For a tradie or a small operator it is the quoting tool, the invoice book and the job list in one, so when it stops working the work stops with it. We treat those as priority jobs and tell you the turnaround before you leave it.

Machines that move between the house and a site pick up their own kind of damage: grit in the ports, a charging socket worn loose, a screen cracked by something heavy on the passenger seat. Each is a part-and-labour job, and we confirm the exact part for your model before ordering it.

If the laptop looks completely dead, bring the charger too. A failed charger is a common culprit, and having it on the bench rules it in or out in minutes.

desktops_intro: |

Plenty of Maddington desktops do double duty: the family computer in the evening and the bookkeeping machine for a small business during the day. When one of those slows to a crawl, it is rarely worn out as a whole. More often one part has fallen behind, and fixing that part costs far less than replacing the tower.

Towers on the light industrial side of the suburb, or in a shed or back office, pull in more dust than a desk in a quiet house. We open the case, clear the fans and heatsink, refresh the thermal paste and check the power supply, which is where many sudden shutdowns start.

If the machine runs something the business cannot be without for a day, tell us when you message, and we will plan the repair so it is back when you need it.

data_intro: |

For a lot of Maddington households and small businesses, one computer holds years of records nobody has copied anywhere else: invoices, quotes, tax paperwork, along with the family photos. Recovery is often possible when a drive starts failing, but how much comes back depends on how the drive is behaving when it reaches us.

Clicking, grinding or files that suddenly will not open are the signs to stop. Turn the machine off and leave it off, because running it longer gives the drive more chances to damage what is still readable.

local_area: |

Maddington is bordered by Thornlie to the west, Kenwick to the north, Gosnells to the south and a stretch of light industrial and semi-rural land to the east toward Martin. It sits around 20 kilometres south-east of the Perth CBD.

The suburb has its own train station on the Armadale line, along with the Maddington Central and Maddington Village shopping centres, and families near Maddington Primary School, East Maddington Primary School or Yule Brook College all sit within easy reach of Spencer Road. Peace Park, Penn Street Reserve and Maddington Oval cover most of the suburb's open space.
