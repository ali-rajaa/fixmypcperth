Queens Park has one of the more genuinely mixed populations of any suburb we cover. Its top ancestries include English, Chinese, Australian, Indian, Nepali and Filipino, a real reflection of decades of migration into this part of Perth, layered onto a suburb that only became fully residential in the post-war boom of the 1950s and 60s, after starting out as poultry farms, market gardens and orchards under a different name.

Directly to the north sits Welshpool, one of Perth's largest industrial estates, and that neighbour shapes a real share of what comes through our door: machines belonging to small businesses and tradespeople working out of the industrial area, alongside the family computers you would expect from a residential suburb its size. The suburb has also grown quickly in recent years, up more than a third since 2011, so there is a genuine mix of older post-war homes and newer households settling in.

That combination, working machines that cannot sit idle and family computers used by several generations under one roof, means the range of what we are asked to fix here is wider than most suburbs on our list.

A genuine share of Queens Park's laptops are work tools for people running a business or trade out of Welshpool, and we treat those as priority jobs, since a laptop going down for a small operator can mean a day of lost invoicing or missed quotes. Outside of that, the usual wear-and-tear faults show up: batteries that no longer hold a charge, fans loud enough to notice, and the occasional cracked screen.

Queens Park households are often multi-generational, several family members sharing devices under one roof, so we see a genuine mix of laptops set up for very different uses on the same machine, work, school, and everything in between.

Bring the charger along as well if the laptop will not power on. It rules out a common false alarm before we even open the case.

desktops_intro: |

Queens Park's older housing stock, much of it dating to the suburb's post-war growth through the 1950s and 60s, means a real share of its desktops are a decade old or more, carrying a mechanical drive that has slowed the whole machine down over the years. Migrating to an SSD is usually the single biggest improvement available, at a fraction of the cost of a new computer.

Heat is the other common fault, particularly on towers that have sat under a desk collecting dust for years. Once the thermal paste dries out and the heatsink clogs, the machine starts cutting out under any real load. A clean and fresh paste generally resolves it in an afternoon.

For a business machine that cannot leave the premises, on-site visits are available across Queens Park, and remote support covers anything that does not need hands physically on the computer.

data_intro: |

Some of the hardest calls we take are from people who assumed their files were safe until a drive proved otherwise, family photos spanning several generations under one roof, or years of records for a business run out of Welshpool. What can be recovered depends on the drive's condition. Still spinning but struggling gives good odds, particularly if it is imaged early. Stopped entirely is a harder job, and the odds drop from there.

Whichever it turns out to be, switching the machine off the moment files start disappearing matters more than anything else, since every extra minute it runs risks overwriting what is still recoverable.
