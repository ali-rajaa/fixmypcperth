why_local: |

Leeming sits in an unusual spot administratively, split across the City of Melville, the City of Cockburn and the City of Canning, and it borders Murdoch, home to Murdoch University and the Fiona Stanley and St John of God hospital precinct. That neighbour shapes the suburb more than most: alongside around 10,900 residents in established family homes, Leeming has a genuine share of university students and shift-working healthcare staff living nearby or passing through.

That mix brings a particular kind of pressure we do not see as much elsewhere. A student's laptop dying two days before an assignment is due, or a nurse coming off a night shift needing their laptop working before the next one starts, are not problems that can wait for a convenient week. Alongside that is the more familiar pattern from an established residential suburb: family desktops that have been in the same house for years, now slower than they used to be.

For the university and healthcare side of that mix especially, the timeline matters as much as the fix itself, and we treat it that way.

A meaningful share of Leeming's laptops belong to people for whom the machine cannot simply wait, university students mid-semester and healthcare staff working shifts at the hospitals nearby. For that group, we try to be upfront about realistic timing before anything else, since a vague answer is worse than a specific one when there is an assignment or a shift on the other side of it.

Outside of that pressure, the usual wear-and-tear faults show up the same as anywhere: batteries that no longer hold charge through a full day of lectures or a shift, and the occasional cracked screen or spilled coffee. Physical damage is priced on the part, confirmed before we order it.

If a laptop looks dead, bring the charger. A failed charger gets mistaken for a dead laptop often enough that having yours with us rules it out in minutes.

desktops_intro: |

Leeming's desktops are mostly the family machines you would expect from an established residential suburb, computers that have sat in the same house for a decade or more with a mechanical drive slowly dragging the whole thing down. Migrating that drive to an SSD is usually the single biggest improvement available, at a fraction of the cost of a new machine.

Overheating comes up less often here than in suburbs with more home-built gaming rigs, but when it does, a clean and fresh thermal paste generally resolves it in an afternoon rather than anything more involved.

If you are not in a position to bring a tower in, on-site visits are available across Leeming, and remote support covers anything that does not need hands physically on the machine.

data_intro: |

Some of the hardest calls we take are from students who have lost a thesis or a semester's worth of assignments with no second copy anywhere, or families whose only photos of the kids growing up lived on one drive. What can be recovered depends on the state of the drive itself. Still spinning but struggling gives a genuinely good chance, particularly if it is imaged before it gets worse. Stopped completely is a harder case, and the odds drop from there.

Whichever it turns out to be, switching the machine off the moment files start disappearing matters more than anything else, since every extra minute it runs risks overwriting what is still recoverable. For anyone relying on a laptop for study, it is also worth setting up a proper cloud backup once it is back, so a single failed drive never costs a whole semester again.
