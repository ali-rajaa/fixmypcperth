why_local: |

Huntingdale was built out during the housing boom of the 1970s and expanded again through the 1990s. It is a settled residential suburb in the City of Gosnells, around 9,000 people across roughly five square kilometres, and the overwhelming majority of households are families who own the place they live in.

That history shows up in the machines. Huntingdale is not a suburb of brand new estates full of year-old laptops. It is households that have had the same desktop under the study desk since the kids were at Huntingdale Primary, and home offices running hardware bought back when working from home was still unusual. The most common thing we are asked about from Huntingdale is not a dramatic failure at all. It is a computer that has slowly become unbearable to use.

That matters because the fix is usually far cheaper than people expect. A machine taking four minutes to reach the desktop is very often an ageing mechanical hard drive that has simply reached the end of its useful speed, not a computer that needs replacing. The same goes for the overheating and sudden-shutdown complaints we get from older towers that have spent a decade quietly filling with dust. Replacing a working computer because it feels slow is the expensive answer to a cheap problem.

Laptops make up a good share of the work that comes to us from Huntingdale, and the faults split fairly neatly into two groups: things that broke because something happened to the machine, and things that wore out.

The ones that broke are cracked screens, snapped hinges, keyboards that met a cup of tea, and charging ports that have been pulled sideways one too many times. These are physical repairs and the part decides the price, so we quote the part before ordering it. The ones that wore out are batteries that no longer hold a charge, fans that have become loud enough to notice, and drives on their way out.

One thing worth knowing before you come in: bring the charger. A surprising share of the laptops brought in as dead turn out to have a failed charger rather than a failed laptop, and having yours on hand saves a step in the diagnosis.

desktops_intro: |

Desktops are the older half of what comes out of Huntingdale, and they are also the ones most worth repairing. A tower from 2015 with a mechanical drive and 8GB of RAM is not a machine at the end of its life. It is a machine with a slow drive. Migrating it to an SSD typically makes more difference to how it feels day to day than any other single change, and it costs a fraction of replacing the computer.

The other common desktop job is heat. Towers sit on carpet, under desks, pulling dust in for years. Eventually the thermal paste dries out, the heatsink clogs, and the machine starts shutting itself off under load with no warning. A deep clean and fresh paste fixes it, and it is an afternoon rather than a rebuild.

If you are not in a position to lift a tower into the car, on-site visits are available across Huntingdale, and remote support covers anything that does not need hands on the machine at all.

data_intro: |

The hardest conversations we have are with people whose only copy of something was on a drive that has stopped working. Family photos, tax records, a small business's invoicing history. Recovery is often possible, but it is never guaranteed and it always costs more than a backup would have.

There are two broad cases. A drive that still powers up but is failing has a good success rate, and the sooner it is imaged the better. A drive that has stopped spinning entirely is harder, and the odds drop. In both cases the single most important thing you can do is stop using the machine the moment you notice files are missing, because every minute it keeps running risks overwriting what is still there.
