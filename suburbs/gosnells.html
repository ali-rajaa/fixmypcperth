why_local: |

Gosnells is one of the oldest suburbs in Perth's south-east, established in 1890 and built out long before most of the suburbs around it. It is the actual town centre of the wider district: the Gosnells Railway Station, the Gosnells Railway Markets, two shopping centres and the City of Gosnells council offices and museum all sit within the suburb itself, alongside around 21,000 residents across roughly 15 square kilometres.

That mix of history and civic infrastructure shows up in what we are asked to look at. A good share of Gosnells machines are the same story as an older, settled family suburb: a desktop that has been in the same house for a decade, now taking minutes to reach the desktop. But being the town centre also means Gosnells sends us more small business and home office machines than a purely residential suburb does, the computer running the till at a shop near the railway markets, or the laptop a tradie based locally uses for invoicing and quotes between jobs.

Both groups tend to make the same mistake: assuming the machine itself is worn out rather than one part of it. A ten-year-old desktop with a slow mechanical drive is not a ten-year-old computer once that drive is replaced, it is a computer that starts in seconds again. Replacing it outright is usually the more expensive option, not the safer one.

Laptops from Gosnells split fairly evenly between two groups: machines that got old, and machines that got knocked around. The ones that got old are usually batteries that no longer hold a charge or fans that have become loud enough to notice. The ones that got knocked around are cracked screens, snapped hinges and keyboards that met a spilled drink, common on machines that travel to and from a shop or a job site rather than staying on a desk at home.

Physical damage is priced on the part, so we confirm the exact panel or component before ordering it rather than quoting blind. Wear-and-tear faults, batteries and fans mostly, are usually a flat labour cost since the part itself is standard across most models.

Bring the charger with you if you can. It sounds like a small thing, but enough laptops come in as "dead" that turn out to just have a failed charger that having yours on the bench saves a step in working out what is actually wrong.

desktops_intro: |

Desktops are where Gosnells' age as a suburb shows up most clearly. A tower bought a decade or more ago with a mechanical drive and 8GB of RAM is not a machine at the end of its life, it is a machine with a slow drive. Migrating it to an SSD typically makes more difference to how it feels day to day than any other single change, and it costs a fraction of replacing the computer outright.

Heat is the other fault that comes up constantly on Gosnells desktops. Years of dust choke the heatsink until the machine starts cutting out under anything more demanding than idling. Clearing it out and fitting fresh thermal paste is a same-day fix, not a rebuild.

If you are not in a position to bring a tower in, on-site visits are available across Gosnells, and remote support covers anything that does not need hands on the machine at all.

data_intro: |

Some of the hardest calls we take are from people who assumed their files were safe right up until the drive proved otherwise. Family photos with no second copy, or years of records for a local business. What can be pulled back depends entirely on the state the drive is in: one that still spins but is struggling gives good odds, especially if it is imaged early. One that has stopped spinning altogether is a harder job, and the odds drop from there.

Whichever it turns out to be, the one thing that helps most is switching the machine off the moment files start disappearing, rather than trying again to see if it was a one-off. Every extra minute it runs is a minute more that can be overwritten.
