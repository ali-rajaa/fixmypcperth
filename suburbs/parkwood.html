why_local: |

Parkwood is small and settled. Established in 1965, it covers under four square kilometres and has stayed close to 6,000 residents for years rather than growing the way its larger neighbours have. The median age is 42, noticeably older than most of the suburbs we cover, and it has a reputation locally as a quiet, established place that a good number of retirees call home alongside longtime families, with Whaleback Golf Course a well-known part of that.

That age profile changes the kind of jobs we see. Rather than the usual gaming rigs and remote-work laptops, a lot of what comes out of Parkwood is a desktop or laptop that gets used lightly, for email, online banking, video calls with family, and browsing, and that has slowed down or stopped behaving the way it used to without much warning. Scam pop-ups and phishing emails aimed specifically at less tech-confident users are a more common reason for a Parkwood visit than they are elsewhere, and a forgotten password locking someone out of their own machine comes up often enough to be one of our regular jobs.

None of this means the fixes are any different or any more complicated. It usually just means explaining what actually happened in plain terms matters as much as the repair itself.

Laptops from Parkwood are less likely than most to be gaming machines or heavily used work tools, and more likely to be the one device in the house used for email, banking and keeping in touch with family. That changes what tends to go wrong: batteries lasting less through a light day of use, and software clutter that builds up slowly and unnoticed rather than a machine being pushed hard.

Where damage does come up, it is often a screen or hinge on a laptop that has been in daily use for years rather than one that gets knocked around. The part decides the price, so we confirm exactly what is needed before ordering anything.

If a laptop looks dead, bring the charger along. A failed charger gets mistaken for a dead laptop more often than you would think, and having yours with us settles that in minutes.

desktops_intro: |

A Parkwood desktop is often the same machine that has sat in the same spot for a decade or more, doing the same light jobs the whole time. That steady use is gentle on the hardware, but the mechanical drive inside still ages the same way it would anywhere else, and it is almost always the reason the machine feels noticeably slower than it did a few years ago.

Migrating that drive to an SSD is the single change most likely to make an older Parkwood desktop feel new again, and it costs a fraction of buying a replacement. Overheating is less common here than in suburbs with more demanding home-built machines, but a clean and fresh thermal paste still resolves it in an afternoon when it does come up.

On-site visits are available across Parkwood for anyone not able to bring a tower in, and remote support covers anything that does not need hands physically on the machine.

data_intro: |

Losing years of family photos or financial records to a failed drive is one of the harder conversations we have, and it comes up in Parkwood as often as anywhere. What can be recovered depends on the drive's condition: one that still spins but is struggling gives a genuinely good chance, especially imaged early. One that has stopped completely is a tougher job, with lower odds either way.

Whichever it turns out to be, the single most useful thing you can do is turn the machine off the moment files start going missing rather than trying again to see if it sorts itself out. Every extra minute it runs is a chance to lose what is still there.

local_area: |

Parkwood is a small suburb bordered by Willetton to the north, Riverton and Ferndale to the north east, Langford and Lynwood to the east and Canning Vale directly to the south and west. It sits around 15 kilometres south-east of the Perth CBD.

Whaleback Golf Course and Hossack Reserve cover much of the suburb's open space, and Parkwood Village Shopping Centre handles most everyday errands without needing to leave the area, with Carousel Shopping Centre a short drive further for anything bigger. Bus routes connect to the Thornlie train line for anyone heading into the city.
