why_local: |

Riverton is small, settled against the Canning River, and shaped by something a lot of families move there for deliberately: school zoning. The suburb has no secondary school of its own, and a good number of Riverton households bought specifically to land inside the Rossmoyne Senior High School catchment, one of the more sought-after public school zones in Perth's south. That gives the suburb a particular kind of stability, families who planned a move years ahead and then stayed.

What we see from Riverton reflects that: family computers that get used hard for actual schoolwork, not just browsing, alongside the more familiar pattern of an established suburb where the household desktop has been in the same spot for years. Riverton's population has also grown noticeably in the past few years as older homes get renovated or rebuilt by young families moving in specifically for the schools, which means a mix of genuinely new machines alongside the older ones.

The river itself matters too, in a small way: a fair few Riverton homes back onto reserve or river frontage, and machines that live in a study or garage near that dampness sometimes show it in ways a drier inland home would not.

A larger share of Riverton's laptops are doing genuine daily school or study duty than in a lot of suburbs, given how many families moved here specifically for the local high school zoning. That changes what actually matters when one comes in: a laptop that is slow but working is often more urgent to a Riverton household mid-term than a machine that has stopped completely but belongs to nobody in exam season.

Outside of that, the usual faults apply: batteries that no longer last a full school day, and the occasional cracked screen from a laptop carried in a bag every morning. What a screen or component costs depends entirely on the exact model, so we check before quoting anything.

Bring the charger along too, if you can. It rules out one of the most common reasons a "dead" laptop turns out not to be dead at all.

desktops_intro: |

Riverton's desktops split between two groups, the older machines that have sat in a settled family home for a decade or more, and newer ones bought by families who have recently moved in or renovated for the schools. The older group carries the familiar mechanical-drive slowdown, and migrating to an SSD is usually the single biggest improvement available.

Where a machine sits near the river, in a garage or a study that gets more damp than the rest of the house, we check for corrosion and connector wear as part of any diagnostic, not just whatever the obvious symptom happens to be.

On-site visits are available across Riverton for anyone not able to bring a tower in, and remote support covers anything that does not need hands physically on the machine.

data_intro: |

Losing a semester of assignments or years of family photos to a failed drive is one of the harder conversations we have, and it comes up in Riverton as often as anywhere. What can be pulled back depends on the drive's condition: still spinning but struggling gives a genuinely good chance, especially imaged early. Stopped completely is a tougher job, and the odds drop from there.

Whichever it turns out to be, switching the machine off the moment files start disappearing matters more than anything else you can do, since every extra minute it runs is a chance to lose what is still there.

Riverton sits on the Canning River, bordered by Willetton and Shelley to the west, Wilson to the north, Ferndale to the east and Parkwood and Canning Vale to the south. It is about 14 kilometres south-east of the Perth CBD.

Riverton Forum, in adjoining Parkwood, and the Riverton Leisureplex cover most shopping and recreation needs, and families near Riverton Primary School or Queen of Apostles School are within the same short trip along High Road. The suburb feeds into both Rossmoyne Senior High School and Lynwood Senior High School for secondary, which is a large part of why families move here in the first place.
