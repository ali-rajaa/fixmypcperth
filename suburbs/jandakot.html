why_local: |

Jandakot is not like the suburbs around it. Its 16 square kilometres hold only about 2,500 residents, because so much of the land is given over to other things. The largest is Jandakot Airport, the busiest general aviation airport in Australia and a base for pilot training and charter flights. Around it sit business parks and light industrial estates full of offices, warehouses and workshops.

The homes that are here tend to sit on large blocks, many of them semi-rural, alongside the bushland of Jandakot Regional Park. The median age is about 45, older than the suburbs next door, and most people own the place they live in.

That mix produces two quite different kinds of computer problem. For businesses, the issue is downtime: an office PC or a shared file that stops working costs money for every hour it stays down. For households on bigger blocks, it is distance. A router in the house does not reach the shed, the studio or the granny flat at the other end of the property.

desktops_intro: |

Office desktops in Jandakot's business parks usually run all day, every day, and they are often older than anyone realises, because nobody wanted to interrupt work to replace them. They keep going until an update or a failing drive stops them without warning.

The better approach is to service them before that happens: clean them out, check the drives, clear the clutter and make sure the important files are backed up somewhere else. That turns a surprise outage into a planned half hour.

For a business with several computers, our business IT plans cover a set number of machines for a monthly fee, with remote support and on-site visits included, so problems get caught early instead of at the worst moment.

data_intro: |

For a business, lost data is not just inconvenient. Quotes, invoices, job records, customer details and the accounts can all live on one machine or one shared drive, and losing them can mean lost work, lost income and a painful few weeks rebuilding records.

Most small businesses believe they have a backup. Far fewer have ever checked that it can actually be restored, and a backup that has quietly stopped running months ago only reveals itself on the day it is needed. Testing a restore is the only way to know for sure.

The link with Canning Vale runs deeper than proximity: the land that is now Canning Vale was known locally as North Jandakot until 1925.
