Battery Warning Signs

Signs Your Laptop Battery Needs Replacing

Laptop batteries wear out gradually, and most people keep using a failing battery long after it should have been replaced. If you recognise any of the following, your battery has reached the end of its useful life and is worth addressing before it gets worse.

Only Works While Plugged In
The laptop switches off the moment you unplug it, even with the battery showing charge. This means the battery can no longer hold or deliver power and needs replacing.
Swollen or Bulging Case
If the trackpad has lifted, the case no longer sits flat, or you can see the bottom panel bulging, the battery is swelling. Stop using the laptop and get it looked at immediately.
Draining Very Fast
A battery that used to last hours now drops from full charge to empty in under an hour, even with light use. This is a clear sign the cells have degraded.
Random Shutdowns
The laptop turns off unexpectedly, sometimes while showing 20 or 30 percent charge remaining. This happens when the battery can no longer deliver a stable voltage under load.
Battery Percentage Jumps Around
The reported charge level jumps from 40 percent straight to 10 percent, or the laptop shuts down without warning it was low. This points to a battery that can no longer report its charge accurately.
Windows Battery Warning
Windows shows a message such as "consider replacing your battery" or the battery icon has a warning symbol. Windows tracks battery health and this message means it has detected genuine degradation.
Laptop Runs Hot Near the Battery
Unusual heat coming from the underside of the laptop near the battery compartment can indicate a battery under stress, which is both a performance and safety concern.
Three to Four Years Old
Laptop batteries typically degrade noticeably after three to four years of regular use, even without any obvious fault. If your laptop is this age and feels less reliable on battery, it is worth checking.

Why It Matters

Why It Is Worth Replacing Your Battery

A failing battery is easy to put off dealing with, especially if the laptop still works while plugged in. Here is why it is worth sorting sooner rather than later.

A swollen battery is a genuine safety risk
  • Swollen lithium batteries can rupture, leak, or in rare cases catch fire if left in use
  • The pressure from swelling can crack the trackpad, warp the case, or damage the screen if the lid is closed on a bulging battery
  • Continued swelling can push against and damage the motherboard and other internal components

If your battery is visibly swollen, stop using the laptop immediately. Do not press down on the case, do not charge it, and bring it in as soon as you can. We handle swollen batteries with proper care and safe disposal.

It restores the portability you paid for. A laptop that only works plugged into the wall is really a desktop with a screen attached. A new battery gets your actual mobility back, whether that is working from a coffee shop, moving around the house, or using it away from a power point during a blackout or when travelling.

It is almost always cheaper than replacing the laptop. Outside of a battery fault, most laptops three to five years old still have plenty of useful life left in the processor, RAM and storage. Replacing a $89 to $200 battery is a fraction of the cost of a new machine, and keeps a perfectly good laptop useful for years longer.

It protects the rest of the laptop. A failing or swelling battery does not just affect battery life. Left unaddressed, it can damage the trackpad, warp the case, put pressure on the motherboard, or in the case of severe swelling, damage the screen if the lid is closed on it. Replacing the battery early avoids this knock on damage.

It removes the daily frustration of managing a dying battery. Constantly hunting for a power outlet, watching the percentage drop faster than it should, or having the laptop shut down mid task is a genuine drain on productivity. A new genuine battery removes that problem entirely rather than working around it.
