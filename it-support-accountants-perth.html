Why IT Failures Hit Accounting Practices Harder Than Most

For most businesses, a few hours of IT downtime is inconvenient. For an accounting firm facing a lodgement deadline or processing a payroll run, those same hours can mean a late submission, a penalty, or a very difficult conversation with a client. The dependency that accountants and bookkeepers have on specific software platforms, reliable internet connectivity, and secure access to cloud portals is higher than almost any other small business category.

There is also the matter of data sensitivity. An accounting firm holds client tax file numbers, bank account details, financial statements, and business records. Under the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles, firms holding this data have specific obligations around how it is stored, secured, and backed up. A data breach caused by inadequate IT security is not just an operational problem for an accounting practice. It is a regulatory and reputational one with consequences that can extend well beyond the cost of the incident itself.

Finally, accounting practices tend to grow their software stack over time, layering cloud platforms, desktop applications, and integrations in ways that create complex dependencies. When an update to one application breaks a connection to another, or when a new staff member needs access configured across five different platforms, the time involved in resolving these issues without proper IT support is genuinely significant. It is time that should be spent on client work, not on troubleshooting software configurations.

The Most Common IT Problems We See in Perth Accounting Firms

After working with accounting and bookkeeping practices across Perth and the southern suburbs, the same issues appear repeatedly. Understanding them is the first step to eliminating them from your practice.

  • !MYOB or Xero access issues caused by failed updates, licence configuration problems, or network settings that conflict with cloud platform requirements, typically surfacing at the worst possible moment during a client deadline period
  • !ATO portal and myGovID connectivity problems where software updates or Windows changes have disrupted the authentication setup needed to lodge returns and access ATO systems
  • !No reliable backup of client financial records, meaning that if a computer fails or a file is accidentally overwritten, there is no recoverable version of that data and the work must be recreated from scratch
  • !Slow computers that affect productivity during peak periods, particularly end of financial year and tax season when multiple applications are running simultaneously and every delay compounds across dozens of client files
  • !Inadequate security on staff devices leaving client financial data exposed to malware, phishing attacks, or theft without the practice being aware of the risk until an incident occurs
  • !New staff onboarding taking too long because there is no documented process for setting up software licences, cloud access, email accounts, and security configurations across all the platforms a new accountant or bookkeeper needs to do their job

Every one of these problems is preventable with the right IT support arrangement in place. None of them should be absorbing time that your practice could be spending on client work.

Data Security and Privacy for Accounting Practices

Accounting firms hold some of the most sensitive personal and financial data of any business category. Tax file numbers, bank account details, superannuation information, company financials, and individual income data are all held under obligations imposed by the Privacy Act 1988, the Australian Privacy Principles, and the ATO's own data security requirements for registered tax agents.

Meeting those obligations is not just about having antivirus software installed. It requires a layered approach covering device security, network security, encrypted backup, access controls, and documented recovery procedures. FixMyPC Perth implements all of these as standard components of every managed IT plan, and can provide documentation of the security measures in place if your practice is required to demonstrate compliance to a regulator or a professional indemnity insurer.

Phishing attacks targeting accounting firms are increasingly common and increasingly sophisticated. Emails impersonating the ATO, ASIC, and major banks are used to steal myGovID credentials and accounting software login details. We include email security guidance, two-factor authentication configuration, and staff awareness advising as part of our onboarding process for every accounting practice client.
