What goes wrong in a warehouse

The failures in this sector are specific and they repeat. Almost all of them are preventable with the right arrangement in place.

  • !Scanners dropping off the wifi mid-pick because racking is a wall of metal and a network that tested fine in an empty shed stops reaching the back aisles once the racks are full
  • !Label and despatch printers going offline after a driver update or an IP change, usually at the worst possible point in the afternoon run
  • !The warehouse management system slowing to a crawl when the database sits on an ageing server under a desk that nobody has looked at in three years
  • !One despatch PC that everything depends on with no documented build, no spare, and no recent backup, so a failed drive costs a day rather than an hour
  • !Office and floor on the same flat network so anything that gets onto the guest or staff wifi has a clear path to the systems running the operation
  • !Stock and sales history living only on the system that generated it with backup retention set to whatever the software defaulted to, which is usually well short of what the ATO expects

Records, retention and what the ATO expects

The ATO requires most business records to be kept for 5 years, and if you trade through a company, section 286 of the Corporations Act 2001 requires financial records for 7 years. For a distribution business that means stock movements, invoices, despatch records and the reports your system generates, all of which usually live in one place.

If you handle food, the retention question gets sharper. Traceability records under the Food Standards Code have to show one step back and one step forward for every consignment, and that only works if the system holding them is still readable. Dangerous goods and freight documentation carries its own record requirements too.

The question worth asking: if the server running your warehouse system failed tonight, how long until you could despatch again, and could you still produce five years of stock records? For a lot of operations the honest answer is that nobody has tested it. That is a different problem from not having a backup, and it is the one that hurts.

We set retention to match the obligation rather than the default, test that the data actually restores, and keep a documented picture of how the operation is put together so a rebuild is a few hours rather than a guess. We are not accountants or food safety auditors, and we do not certify compliance. We make sure the systems holding your records are not the weak point.
