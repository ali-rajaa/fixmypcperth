Why IT Failures Are Particularly Costly for Real Estate Agencies

A real estate agency is a data-driven business running on tight legal deadlines. Trust accounting errors carry regulatory consequences under the Real Estate and Business Agents Act. Missed inspection records create liability. Unsigned lease documents delay occupancy and cost landlord clients rental income. Every hour of IT downtime in a real estate office translates into a measurable financial and reputational cost that cannot simply be recovered by working longer the next day.

Real estate agencies also carry significant compliance obligations under the Australian Privacy Act with respect to the personal and financial information they hold for tenants, landlords and buyers. A security breach affecting client data is not just a reputational problem. It is a regulatory one. Agencies that do not have properly managed antivirus, device security and backup arrangements in place are exposed to risks that a single ransomware incident could make very real.

Staff turnover in real estate is higher than most industries. New property managers and sales staff join regularly, each needing a correctly configured device with access to the right software, cloud accounts and internal systems from day one. Without a structured IT arrangement, each new starter costs hours of setup time that falls on whoever in the office is most technically capable, which is rarely the most productive use of their time.

What goes wrong in a real estate agency

After working with real estate businesses across Perth and the southern suburbs, the same IT problems appear repeatedly. Understanding them is the first step toward making sure they stop affecting your agency.

  • ! Property management software going down
    PropertyMe, Console Cloud and PropertyTree outages are often caused by Windows update conflicts or network misconfigurations that were never properly addressed during initial setup. The software gets blamed when the underlying IT environment is the real cause.
  • ! Trust accounting software errors
    Rockend, REST Professional and Console trust accounting tools require consistent environments across all devices. Driver conflicts, outdated software versions and incorrect user permissions generate accounting errors that take hours to trace and correct at end of month.
  • ! DocuSign and digital signing failures
    Digital lease and contract signing through DocuSign and REI Forms Live fails when browser extensions conflict, security certificates expire or network settings block the required connections. This holds up lease signings and settlement completions at the worst possible moments.
  • ! Cloud file access problems in the field
    Property managers accessing inspection reports, tenancy agreements and condition reports from tablets or phones in the field encounter access problems when OneDrive or SharePoint synchronisation settings were never correctly configured across all devices.
  • ! Slow office computers affecting productivity
    Aging or poorly maintained computers slow down quoting, database searches and document processing across the office. The time lost each week across an entire team is rarely measured but consistently costs more than the IT support that would prevent it.
  • ! No secure backup of critical records
    Tenancy files, inspection records, maintenance history, landlord communications and financial records stored only on local drives or desktop folders are unprotected. A single hardware failure, theft or ransomware attack can permanently destroy records that carry legal obligations to retain.

Every one of these problems is preventable with the right IT support in place. None of them should be absorbing your team's time during a working day.

Trust account records, audits and the new AML obligations

Agencies in Western Australia hold trust money under the Real Estate and Business Agents Act 1978 and the Real Estate and Business Agents (General) Regulations 1979. The Act requires a formal set of trust records that can show at any time the state of the account, and section 70 requires those accounts to be audited to the standard set by the Commissioner for Consumer Protection.

The audit has a hard deadline. It must be completed and lodged within three months of the end of each calendar year, so 31 March. Missing it is not treated lightly: in 2025 a Bicton agency was fined $14,000 for overdue audits across 2021, 2022 and 2023, and the penalty range in WA extends to suspension or cancellation of the licence.

From 1 July 2026 there is a second layer. The federal AML/CTF regime now covers real estate professionals, which adds customer due diligence and suspicious matter reporting to what you already keep for AUSTRAC. In practice that means more identity documents, held for longer, on the same systems that were already carrying trust records and tenancy files.

What this asks of your IT: records that can be produced on demand rather than reconstructed, so the audit trail survives a failed drive or a staff departure. Per-user logins so entries in PropertyMe, Console or REST are attributable. Retention long enough to cover the audit period rather than whatever the backup product defaulted to. And real access control now that identity documents are sitting in your system.

We handle that side of it: tested backups with retention set to the obligation, access control, patching and monitored antivirus on every machine touching trust or client data. We are not auditors or AML advisers and we do not certify compliance. We make sure your systems are not the weak point when someone asks for the records.
